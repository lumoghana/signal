## Editing for clarity

The illustration uses the same student, phone pose, green Lumo frame and mirror-only expenditure objects. The green ring, confident reflected textile and interior commitments are preserved. The entire real student outside the ring remains monochrome; colour is revealed only in the reflected ochre/navy/cream print and green frame. The quiet Legon setting is retained. This is a Home-specific hierarchy refinement within the established illustration family.

Money Story now uses one caption, “Your money story · last 7 days,” and one observation: “Food took GH₵121.50 of GH₵241.50 paid out.” It has no separate supporting line or bold secondary headline. Transfers explicitly describe money sent, ties show equal amounts, and single-category totals appear once. Its former bar and category grid remain off Home. The full category list, dates, recent payments, calculations/scope and working actions remain in the same one-tap detail sheet. Fees are included once; pending/failed/reversed/incoming payments remain excluded. Empty histories, large amounts and long labels keep natural wording and wrapping.

The main plan-derived amount stays 44px/600. Available balance remains a separately labelled 25px amount, now Regular 400. Removing the internal divider groups these facts with the allocation explanation; the controlled 12px separation remains. Receiving actions are two outlined white/ink utilities with 54px targets and 16px/500 labels; decorative button icons are omitted visually. Home-only hover and focus rules preserve contrast and visible focus. Money Story uses 16px/500 body text under its 13px caption, with one top divider and no lower border. Main figure, action and story text sizes are preserved. Home retains controlled 16px section gaps, reduced to 12px on short viewports, and the flexible illustration absorbs spare height. Content wraps naturally; no fixed text height, clipping or extra required step is introduced. The eye control remains intact.

## Short student check

Use the sample account, without explaining the intended answers first:

1. Ask the student to reveal the amounts and explain available balance versus Left after your plan in their own words. Record misunderstandings, not just whether they say it looks clear.
2. Ask, “What do you think this mirror is showing?” Check whether commitments/impacts are understood without prompting.
3. Ask where the money went, then ask them to open the full breakdown and return. Record the answer, taps, hesitation and required scrolling on their own phone.

Use the findings before assigning a higher design or usability rating. The artwork is a metaphor; the amounts in the interface remain the account-backed financial authority.
